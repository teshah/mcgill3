The Science & Evidence

Dr. Stuart McGill's Laboratory Findings

⏱️ 1. Why 10-Second Holds? (NIRS Oxygenation)

Holding continuous planks for 1–2 minutes causes intramuscular hypoxia (oxygen depletion) and metabolite accumulation, triggering muscle cramps and motor failure.

Using Near-Infrared Spectroscopy (NIRS), Dr. McGill proved that brief 10-second holds separated by 3–5s micro-rests allow blood to rapidly re-perfuse and oxygenate stabilizing muscles, maintaining perfect spinal stiffness without fatigue.

📄 McGill et al. (2000) Ergonomics • DOI: 10.1080/001401300184369 ↗

📉 2. Why the 6-4-2 Descending Pyramid?

Muscles that stabilize the spine are primarily built for endurance, not maximum strength. When core stabilizers are pushed to exhaustion, the body defaults to hazardous compensatory motions, transferring load directly into vulnerable spinal ligaments and discs.

The Russian descending pyramid (6 reps → 4 reps → 2 reps) matches your neuromuscular fatigue profile, allowing you to accumulate high training volume without your form deteriorating.

📄 McGill (2010) Strength & Conditioning J. • DOI: 10.1519/SSC.0b013e3181df4521 ↗

⚠️ 3. Why Not Sit-Ups or Crunches?

Axler & McGill (1997) measured that traditional sit-ups and crunches place over 3,400 N of compressive force across flexed lumbar discs (exceeding safety guidelines from NIOSH).

The McGill Curl-Up places hands under the lumbar lordosis to prevent spine flattening, providing maximal rectus abdominis activation while keeping spinal loads well below safe thresholds.

📄 Axler & McGill (1997) Med Sci Sports Exerc • DOI: 10.1097/00005768-199706000-00011 ↗
